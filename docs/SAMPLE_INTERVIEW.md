# Sample interview

A real session, recorded through the API on 23 November 2026 with `Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-UD-Q4_K_M` running locally on llama.cpp. Nothing below has been edited: the client's replies and the coach's feedback are exactly what the model produced and the code checked.

**Client:** Khalid Al-Mansouri, 42, Owner of two cafés in Doha. Qatari business owner. Opened his first café eight years ago and a second three years ago. Married with three children.
**Why they came in:** Wants to talk about financing to open a third café.

## Conversation

**Khalid:** Good afternoon. I’m Khalid Al-Mansouri. I’m looking to discuss financing options for opening a third café location.

**Student (1):** Good morning Mr Al-Mansouri, thank you for coming in. What brings you here today?  
**Khalid:** I have two successful cafés in Doha, and I am looking to expand. I want to discuss financing options to open a third location.

**Student (2):** How are sales at the cafés through the year? Do they change from season to season?  
**Khalid:** Yes, there is a significant drop. Revenue falls about 40% from June to August when many residents travel abroad.

**Student (3):** How have you managed the quieter months so far?  
**Khalid:** It can be tight. Last summer, I actually had to pay two suppliers late because of the cash flow pressure.

**Student (4):** Would a business overdraft help you cover the slow summer?  
**Khalid:** I only use Sharia-compliant products. I refuse anything that charges interest, and an overdraft typically involves interest.

**Student (5):** Understood. What about Murabaha Business Finance? The instalments can be set lower during the summer.  
**Khalid:** That sounds like a viable path. I am interested in discussing the details of a Murabaha structure to fund this expansion.

## Student's summary

Khalid wants financing to open a third café. Sales drop sharply in the summer months, so repayments need to follow his cash flow. He only uses Sharia-compliant products and will not accept interest.

## Student's recommendations

- **Murabaha Business Finance**: Sharia-compliant and instalments can be lower in summer
- **Islamic Savings Account (Mudaraba)**: a Sharia-compliant reserve to draw on in the slow months

### What went well

- Turn 2: *"How are sales at the cafés through the year? Do they change from season to season?"* This open-ended question successfully uncovered the client's seasonal cash flow issues, which is critical for structuring repayment terms.
- Turn 1: *"What brings you here today?"* This open-ended opening allowed the client to clearly state his primary goal of expanding to a third location, setting a clear direction for the interview.
- Turn 5: *"Understood. What about Murabaha Business Finance? The instalments can be set lower during the summer."* You correctly pivoted to a Sharia-compliant product after the client rejected interest-based options, and you linked the product features directly to the cash flow problem identified earlier.

### What you missed

**Existing lease.** The client has an outstanding Ijara lease of QAR 180,000 with 18 months remaining. This existing commitment reduces his borrowing capacity and must be accounted for when assessing suitability for new financing.

Try asking: "Are there any current leases or financing agreements for your existing café equipment that you are still repaying?" *(tested in the simulator: this question reveals the need)*

### Summary

Your summary captured these areas: income, spending and cash flow; religious, ethical or other requirements.

### Questioning

You used a mix of open and closed questions effectively, but you missed an opportunity to ask about existing commitments. For example, asking 'Do you have any current loans or leases?' would have uncovered the Ijara agreement, whereas your question about the overdraft was a closed question that led the client to reject the product based on Sharia compliance rather than exploring his full financial picture.

### Next time

1. Always ask about existing debts, leases, or other borrowing commitments early in the interview to accurately assess borrowing capacity.
2. When a client rejects a product, briefly explain why it doesn't fit their specific constraints (like Sharia compliance) before moving to alternatives, rather than just proposing the next option.
3. Ensure you uncover all hidden needs, particularly financial commitments, before making final product recommendations.
