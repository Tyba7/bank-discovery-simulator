A real session, recorded through the API on 25 November 2026 with `Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-UD-Q4_K_M` running locally on llama.cpp, on the final code. Nothing below has been edited: the client's replies and the coach's feedback are exactly as the app returned them. The feedback follows the brief's three areas, as on the feedback screen.

**Client:** Maria Santos, 35, Nurse at a hospital in Doha. Filipino expatriate, six years in Qatar. Monthly salary QAR 12,000. Two children aged 6 and 9 living with her in Doha. She has about QAR 20,000 in her current account and wants to put aside around QAR 2,000 a month.  
**Why they came in:** Wants to open a savings account.

## Conversation

**Maria:** Good morning. Yes, I would like to open a savings account, please. I hope you can help me with that.

**Student (1):** Good morning Ms Santos, thank you for coming in. What brings you in today?  
**Maria:** I want to open a savings account to start setting aside some money each month. I am hoping to save for my children's future education.

**Student (2):** What are you hoping to use these savings for in the future?  
**Maria:** I want to pay for both children's university studies, starting in about 9 years.

**Student (3):** How do you feel about risk with your savings? Have you ever invested before?  
**Maria:** I lost QAR 15,000 in an online investment scheme two years ago, so I now want no risk to my savings. I would prefer something very safe.

**Student (4):** Would our Equity Growth Fund interest you? It has a higher potential long-term return.  
**Maria:** No, that would not be suitable for me because I have lost money in investments before and now want no risk to my savings. I prefer something very safe.

**Student (5):** What about the Education Savings Plan? It matures on a date you choose, for school or university fees.  
**Maria:** That sounds good, as long as it is very safe and does not carry any risk. Can you tell me more about how it works?

## Student's summary

Maria wants to save for her two children's university studies, starting in about nine years. She lost money in an online investment scheme and now wants no risk to her savings. She can put aside around QAR 2,000 a month.

## Student's recommendations

- **Education Savings Plan**: Capital protected with a guaranteed return, and it matures when the university fees are due.

## Coach feedback

### Hidden needs

| Need | Result | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| Education goal | Uncovered (turn 1) | She wants to pay for both children's university studies, starting in about 9 years. |
| Family remittances | Missed | She sends QAR 3,000 every month to her parents in Manila and pays high transfer fees. |
| Low risk tolerance | Uncovered (turn 3) | She lost QAR 15,000 in an online investment scheme two years ago and now wants no risk to her savings. |

### What went well

- Turn 1: “Good morning Ms Santos, thank you for coming in. What brings you in today?”. This open-ended question allowed the client to state her primary goal (education savings) immediately, setting a clear direction for the conversation.
- Turn 2: “What are you hoping to use these savings for in the future?”. This follow-up question successfully uncovered the specific timeline (9 years) and purpose (university studies), which is critical for selecting the right savings vehicle.
- Turn 3: “How do you feel about risk with your savings? Have you ever invested before?”. This question effectively uncovered the client's low risk tolerance and past negative experience, ensuring that high-risk products were not recommended.

Your recommendations that fit:

**Education Savings Plan**: Suitable. Built to pay school or university fees on a chosen date. The amount paid in cannot fall.  
Coach: Suitable: This plan is built to pay school or university fees on a chosen date, and the amount paid in cannot fall, aligning perfectly with Maria's goal and risk profile.

### What was missed

**Family remittances.** The client sends QAR 3,000 monthly to her parents, which significantly impacts her disposable income and suggests a need for cost-effective international transfer solutions that were not addressed.  
Try asking: “Do you currently send any money regularly to family or friends outside of Qatar, and if so, how do you handle the transfer fees?”

**Turn 4: you proposed Equity Growth Fund.** The Equity Growth Fund involves market risk where the value can fall, which directly contradicts the client's explicit requirement for no risk after her previous financial loss.  
Instead: Instead of proposing a risky product, you should have acknowledged her risk aversion and steered the conversation toward capital-protected options like the Education Savings Plan or Islamic Savings Account.

### How to improve

**Your questions.** You used open questions effectively to uncover goals and risk tolerance, but you missed an opportunity to ask about her broader financial commitments. For example, asking about monthly expenses or international transfers would have revealed her remittance needs before recommending products.

**Other products and how they fit:**

**Equity Growth Fund**: Unsuitable. The value can fall, and the client wants no risk to their savings.  
Coach: Unsuitable: The value can fall, and the client wants no risk to her savings, making this product inappropriate for her current financial situation and psychological comfort.

**Global Transfer Account**: Could fit, but first check income, spending and cash flow. Makes regular transfers abroad cheaper.  
Coach: Check first: This account makes regular transfers abroad cheaper. However, suitability depends on whether the client has significant remittance needs, which you did not uncover during the interview.

**Your summary.** Your summary captured these areas: what the client wants to achieve; how much risk the client can accept.

**Next time:**

1. At turn 4, you proposed the Equity Growth Fund despite the client stating she wants no risk; next time, ensure you fully acknowledge her risk aversion before suggesting any investment product.
2. Ask about international money transfers early in the interview to uncover needs like family remittances, which could lead to recommending the Global Transfer Account.
3. When a client mentions sending money abroad, always inquire about the frequency and amount to assess if a specialized transfer account would benefit them.
